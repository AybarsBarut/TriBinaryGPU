## 2. M1 Micro-Experiment: Sign Detection vs Full Comparator (Finding 2 / H2)

We conducted a dedicated micro-experiment (`experiments/m1_comparator.yaml`) comparing binary two's complement and balanced ternary at iso-precision widths: 8b vs 6t (9.51 eff bits), 16b vs 11t (17.43 eff bits), 32b vs 21t (33.28 eff bits).

Stimulus: 10,000 paired operations drawn from the common representable signed integer range.

### Results: Sign Detection Alone (`signdet`)

| Iso-Precision Pair | Binary Arch | Ternary Arch | Delay Saving (Primary) | Transistor Saving | Nominal Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **8b vs 6t** (9.51 eff bits) | scan (MSB) | tree (ZSEL) | **-94.1%** | **-150.0%** | **REFUTED** |
| **16b vs 11t** (17.43 eff bits)| scan (MSB) | tree (ZSEL) | **-79.6%** | **-125.8%** | **REFUTED** |
| **32b vs 21t** (33.28 eff bits)| scan (MSB) | tree (ZSEL) | **-96.4%** | **-125.8%** | **REFUTED** |

**Scientific Interpretation:**
In two's complement binary, extracting the sign of an existing number is an $O(1)$ zero-gate operation (simply tapping the MSB wire).
In balanced ternary, the sign is the sign of the **most significant non-zero trit**. If upper trits are zero, they do not dictate the sign. Extracting the sign requires a priority reduction tree (`ZSEL` cells), imposing $O(\log n)$ logic depth and substantial transistor cost.
**The claim that balanced ternary makes standalone sign detection cheaper is decisively REFUTED.**

---

### Results: Full Relational Comparator (`cmp(a, b) = sign(a - b)`)

| Iso-Precision Pair | Binary Arch | Ternary Arch | Delay Saving (Primary) | Transistor Saving | Nominal Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **8b vs 6t** | tree (XOR+NOR) | tree (TCMP+ZSEL)| **+20.7%** | **+33.6%** | **SUPPORTED** |
| **16b vs 11t** | tree (XOR+NOR) | tree (TCMP+ZSEL)| **+22.9%** | **+39.0%** | **SUPPORTED** |
| **32b vs 21t** | tree (XOR+NOR) | tree (TCMP+ZSEL)| **+24.3%** | **+41.6%** | **SUPPORTED** |

**Scientific Interpretation:**
When comparing two distinct numbers ($a$ and $b$), balanced ternary achieves a **>20% critical-path speedup** and **>33% transistor saving** under nominal parameters.
Why? Because the ternary `TCMP` gate computes the full trichotomy ($<, =, >$) at single-digit granularity in a single logic level, and the radix economy allows 21 trits to cover a full 32-bit range, reducing the reduction tree depth compared to binary bit-comparator slices.

## 3. NVIDIA NIM Red-Team Audit Summary

The experiment results were submitted to `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b` via the `RadixLab RedTeamAgent`.
The red-team review reached the following conclusions:
- **Major Finding on Sign-Detection:** The standalone sign-detection result is an unequivocal, severe negative result for balanced ternary (5–10× slower, 4.5× more energy, scaling 3.9× worse per word-width doubling). The report must lead with this reality rather than obscuring it behind comparator gains.
- **Transistor Asymmetry:** Balanced ternary achieves a 33–42% transistor advantage in full comparison (`cmp(a, b)`), but incurs a 125–150% transistor penalty in standalone sign detection. The root cause is the fundamental difference between reading a hardwired MSB wire in two's complement binary vs. dynamically resolving the most significant non-zero trit across an entire word.
- **Granularity Bias Note:** The 8b vs 6t comparison gives ternary 9.51 effective bits (18.8% capacity headroom). Future milestone reporting will include metrics normalized per effective bit to isolate this factor.
